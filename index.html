Selected Work

Too many systems. Not enough answers.

One job can have information in three different programs—and still be missing something important. I built connected tools to help spot what's missing, find the next step, and keep people informed.

See the project and examples

I started with everyday questions: Which jobs are waiting? What paperwork is missing? Has someone updated the customer? Why am I checking the same thing in three different places? Rather than replace everything, I wanted to keep what worked and fix the gaps between the programs.

Better work, not just more software.

Central Job Control (CJC)

Role in the whole: the home base for job information source of truth. CJC brings together intake data, job matching, status logic, source history, approved users, contact information, and manual overrides before information moves into downstream workflows.

Why it exists separately: the core data and business rules need one stable home. Keeping that foundation separate prevents reporting, alerts, and communication tools from becoming competing versions of the same job record.

Daily Action & Notes Builder (DANB)

Role in the whole: helps turn job information into the day's actionable work—standardized notes, follow-up requests, documentation, and next-step decisions.

Why it exists separately: action logic changes more often than source data. DANB can improve the daily workflow without destabilizing the underlying job record or removing human review points.

Target Watch (TW)

Role in the whole: watches target dates, schedule movement, overdue activity, holds, stalled work, and missing target-date reasoning—including structured note patterns used to support downstream checks.

Why it exists separately: schedule surveillance is a specialized control function. Reverse-engineering the existing process made it possible to preserve business rules while adding clearer exception and QA visibility.

Field Communication System (FCS)

Role in the whole: helps provide a communication bridge between office, field, and client-facing work so requests, responses, and operational context are less likely to disappear into scattered texts or calls.

Why it exists separately: communication has a different lifecycle from production data. Separating it keeps the operational record clean while still connecting communication back to the job. Its recorded updates and review trail also create an existing leadership-visibility vein.

PM Action & Alert System

Role in the whole: turns compliance and project needs into smaller prioritized requests for project managers, focusing first on WIP, then Pre-Production, then Pending Sales, while bundling related compliance updates into the same touchpoint.

Why it exists separately: the control must protect both compliance needs and human workload. Smaller batches reduce overload, create an auditable paper trail, and provide evidence for later PDCA-based improvement.

Leadership & Improvement View — Concept

Status in the whole: an idea for a future dashboard concept for turning operational activity into management context. It is not a completed current module; leadership visibility today is supported through FCS communication traceability and source records.

Why retain the concept: leadership ultimately needs decision-ready visibility rather than every transaction. Any future summary must preserve links to the source history used by the operational team.

Human-reviewed by design: AI may assist with organizing information and drafting communication, but source validation, exceptions, approval, and final decisions remain with people.

How I Work

Make it useful on a busy Tuesday.

The written procedure and the way people actually do the work aren't always the same thing. I pay attention to both.

🔎 Ask why

Understand the real process, its users, its constraints, and the governing source of truth.

🧩 Connect the pieces

Create a focused workflow with clear ownership, decision points, exceptions, and handoffs.

🛠️ Build something usable

Test outputs, document controls, prepare users, and keep human approval where it belongs.

🌱 Keep improving

Use results, evidence, and user feedback to strengthen the next iteration.

About

A process person. And a people person.

I like asking questions, especially the ones that start with, “Why do we do it this way?”

Before changing something, I want to know why it worked that way in the first place. Most processes started as someone's sensible answer to a real problem. The work changed, the tools changed, or the rules changed. Now we need to look again.

What matters to me is the whole picture: the customer, the people doing the work, the paperwork, the deadlines, the cost, and the quality of the result. Improving one piece shouldn't make the others worse.

I bring more than 20 years of experience in operations, compliance, quality, documentation, and problem-solving. I use AI where it helps, but I believe people should check the facts and make the decisions.
