Evidence-backed case study

Production Control & Communication System

When information is spread across programs, notes, and messages, just finding out what happened can take hours. These are the tools I built to make the next step easier to see.

Review the evidenceView repository artifacts

Privacy note: All examples below are sanitized or reconstructed. Names, job identifiers, addresses, contacts, claim details, and insurer data are fictional.

Where it started

We were spending too much time looking for answers.

Each example tackles a different part of the same problem. I wanted tools that fit the actual work, not another system people would have to work around.

The problem

Important job details lived in different places. It was too easy to miss a date, note, or follow-up.

What I built

A home base for the information and focused tools for daily tasks, dates, and communication.

Why this helps

It works alongside the programs people already use. No need to replace everything to make one piece better.

A look at each piece

Each piece has its own job. The examples are sanitized or reconstructed, and they show what I was trying to solve.

🧩 Central Job Control (CJC)

This is the home base. It brings job details together and helps catch conflicts before they spread.

Problem

Different sources and identifiers created duplicate entry and conflicting job truth.

Control

Source matching, preserved history, controlled status logic, and visible manual overrides.

Demonstrates

Data governance, business rules, exception handling, and source-of-truth design.

📝 Daily Action & Notes Builder (DANB)

DANB turns a long list of jobs into a manageable day: what's missing, what needs doing, and what to say next.

Problem

Repeated review and note preparation consumed hours and produced inconsistent follow-up.

Control

Focused daily queues with human review, exception handling, and manual override.

Demonstrates

Workflow automation, documentation controls, decision support, and efficiency.

🔎 Target Watch (TW)

Target Watch looks for dates that are getting close, have passed, or don't have a clear explanation. Less hunting, more knowing.

Problem

Target logic lived in habits and note patterns, making schedule surveillance inconsistent.

Control

Structured date patterns, hold recognition, missing-reason QA alerts, and manual review.

Demonstrates

Reverse engineering, requirements discovery, monitoring, and QA controls.

🔔 PM Action & Alert System

People can't act on an avalanche of requests. This design groups important work into smaller batches, puts active jobs first, and follows up on unfinished items.

Problem

Large request dumps overloaded project managers and made urgent items easier to miss.

Control

Stage priority, daily request cap, due-date email, response status, and weekly escalation.

Demonstrates

Workload design, compliance operations, prioritization, and change management.

💬 Field Communication System (FCS)

Field and office updates shouldn't disappear into a text thread. FCS offers two ways to send them in and keeps a record of what happened next.

Problem

Requests and updates were scattered across calls, texts, and office follow-up.

Control

Excel QR generator, structured intake, office review, client-update decision, and recorded closure.

Demonstrates

Communication workflow design, traceability, coordination, and user-centered operations.

🌱 Leadership & Improvement View — Concept

Status: planned standalone view, not a completed current module. The earlier Leadership View belonged to the old Note Engine and has not been developed since the Note Builder moved into DANB. Leadership visibility currently exists through the FCS communication trail and controlled source records.

Eventually, I'd like leaders to see patterns: what's repeatedly late, missing, or stuck. This picture is an idea, not a finished dashboard.

Need

Transactional activity alone does not reveal system health or recurring bottlenecks.

Proposed control

Future summaries should link to source detail and preserve human interpretation.

Concept demonstrates

KPI planning, management-reporting design, and PDCA thinking—not a finished build.

People still make the calls. AI can help sort information and draft updates. Someone still needs to check the facts, solve the exceptions, and decide what happens.
